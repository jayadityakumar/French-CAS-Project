Welcome, and how to use this guide

This guide is organised the way the course is actually taught: five themes, three assessment components, one grade at the end. If you're starting from zero, work through it roughly in order. If you're mid-course and revising for something specific, skip straight to the section you need — nothing here depends on reading the rest first.

Everything is free and there's no account to make. New sections are being added one at a time, so parts of the guide below are still marked "coming soon" — that's genuinely just where the work is up to, not a paywall in disguise.

What is French Ab Initio?

Ab initio means "from the beginning." The course assumes no prior French at all, and builds practical, everyday French across five IB themes: Identities, Experiences, Human Ingenuity, Social Organisation, and Sharing the Planet.

  • Paper 1 — 25%Two short written texts (75–150 words each), such as an email, blog post, or message.
  • Paper 2 — 50%Reading and listening comprehension across a range of short, everyday texts and recordings.
  • Individual Oral — 25%A recorded 12–15 minute conversation with your teacher, built around a visual stimulus.

Marks in every component reward the same underlying things: how fully you complete the task you were set, how accurately and naturally you use the language, and — for the oral in particular — how well you interact, develop an answer, and recover when you get stuck.

Honest advice, from someone doing this right now ↴

  • Learn vocabulary in themes, not random lists — it sticks far better when it's grouped by topic.
  • Say your oral answers out loud, every time. Rehearsing in your head doesn't count.
  • Little and often beats cramming. Fifteen minutes a day for two years beats a panicked week in May.
  • Mistakes while speaking are fine — examiners are marking what you can do, not just what you get wrong.
