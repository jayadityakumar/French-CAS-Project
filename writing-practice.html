Writing — Paper 1

How Paper 1 (writing) works

Structure

Paper 1 is worth 25% of your final grade and lasts 1 hour. You complete two tasks (Task A and Task B), of equal weighting, each answered in 70–150 words. For each task you're given a choice of three prompts, each drawn from a different IB theme — pick whichever one you can write about most confidently.

One personal, one professional

Across the two tasks, one will ask for a personal text (email, blog post, diary entry, postcard) and the other for a professional or mass-media text (formal letter, article, notice, announcement). You choose the specific text type yourself from the options given for that task — so knowing the conventions below matters as much as the language itself.

CriterionWhat it's really asking
LanguageAccuracy and range — correct verbs, agreement, and varied vocabulary, not just a handful of safe phrases.
MessageDid you actually answer the task? Every bullet point in the prompt needs a response.
Conceptual understandingRegister, audience, and text-type conventions — does it read like a real email, not an essay with "Salut" stuck on top?

You're not tested on outside knowledge of a topic — only on how well you communicate within it.

Text types & conventions

Examiners check for these conventions specifically under "conceptual understanding" — get the opening and closing right and you're already partway to a good mark, before a single sentence of content.

Email / message (informal)Writing to a friend or family memberSalut / Coucou / Cher, Chère [prénom]Bisous / À bientôt / Grosse bise

Phrases that earn marks

Before you write

Read the task for its bullet points, not just its topic — each one usually needs its own sentence or two, and missing one costs you Message marks even if your French is flawless. Pick the text type you're most confident writing in, not necessarily the "obvious" one.

Stay inside the word count

70–150 words is short. Padding with filler sentences doesn't help — examiners are marking density of content, not length. If you're well past 150, you're not being asked to write more; you're being asked to say the same thing more precisely.

Model answers

One personal text, one professional — the same pairing you'll get in the real exam. Read the prompt, try writing your own version first, and only then compare against the model.

Task A — Personal text (email)

Prompt

Un(e) ami(e) français(e) t'a invité(e) à passer le week-end chez lui/elle. Écris un email pour accepter l'invitation. Mentionne pourquoi tu es content(e), ce que tu veux faire ensemble, et pose une question sur le week-end. (70–150 mots)

Model answer · 79 motsRéponse à l'invitation

Salut Marion,

Merci beaucoup pour ton invitation ! Je suis vraiment content(e) de venir passer le week-end chez toi, car j'adore ta ville et j'ai hâte de te revoir.

Le samedi, on pourrait se promener dans le centre-ville et visiter le marché. Ensuite, on pourrait regarder un film ou cuisiner ensemble — j'apprends à faire des crêpes en ce moment !

Est-ce que tes parents seront là aussi ? Et qu'est-ce que je dois apporter ?

À bientôt,
Léa

Why this works

Informal greeting and sign-off (Salut / À bientôt) match the email register. Every bullet point from the prompt is covered: reason for being happy (car j'adore ta ville), plans (on pourrait...), and a question about the weekend. Uses a connector (ensuite) and an opinion-adjacent phrase (j'ai hâte de) from the table above, plus a properly conjugated futur-proche-style suggestion with pourrait.

Task B — Professional / mass-media text (article)

Prompt

Ton lycée organise une journée du bénévolat. Écris un article pour le journal du lycée pour encourager les élèves à participer. Explique ce qu'est le bénévolat, pourquoi c'est important, et donne un exemple d'activité. (70–150 mots)

Model answer · 101 motsParticipez à la Journée du Bénévolat !

Le mois prochain, notre lycée organise une journée spéciale dédiée au bénévolat. C'est une excellente occasion de rencontrer nos voisins et d'aider notre communauté.

Le bénévolat, c'est donner de son temps sans être payé — par exemple, nettoyer un parc, aider dans une maison de retraite, ou collecter des vêtements pour les personnes dans le besoin. Cette expérience développe des compétences utiles pour l'avenir.

Cette année, les élèves vont planter des arbres dans le jardin communautaire. À mon avis, c'est un projet formidable pour notre environnement.

Inscrivez-vous avant vendredi auprès de Madame Dubois !

Why this works

A headline instead of a greeting, and no personal sign-off — both correct for an article. Covers all three bullet points: what volunteering is (c'est donner de son temps...), why it matters (développe des compétences utiles), and a concrete example (planter des arbres). Uses par exemple and à mon avis from the phrase table, and closes with a call to action rather than "bisous," which is exactly the register shift an email wouldn't need.

Tip ↴

  • Write your own answer to a prompt before looking at the model — comparing your version afterward teaches you far more than reading a finished answer cold.
  • Getting the text type right (email vs. article vs. formal letter) is worth real marks under "conceptual understanding" — don't default to email for everything just because it's familiar.
  • Count your words once you're done. Consistently landing at 60 or 170 means your planning stage needs work, not your French.
