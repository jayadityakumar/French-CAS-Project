The Individual Oral

10 minutes, recorded, worth 25% of your grade — and the single most commonly lost mark has nothing to do with your French. How the exam actually runs, how to describe an image without just listing objects, the target-culture link examiners are specifically checking for, and how to survive the conversation once the image part is over.

How the Individual Oral works

Structure

The Individual Oral is worth 25% of your final grade. It's an internal assessment — your teacher conducts and records it, and a sample of recordings is sent to the IB for moderation. It's based on the five IB themes.

How it runs

You're shown two visual stimuli, each linked to a different theme and labelled in French with its theme. You choose one. You then get 15 minutes to prepare — you may take brief working notes (around ten short points) into the exam, for reference only; they can't be read aloud as a script.

PartWhat happensRoughly
Part 1 — PresentationYou describe the image and relate it explicitly to its theme and to the target culture.1–2 min
Part 2 — Follow-up discussionYour teacher expands on the theme of your chosen stimulus.3–4 min
Part 3 — General conversationThe conversation moves to at least one different theme from the syllabus.3–4 min

Total recorded time is 10 minutes — the per-part split is approximate and depends on how the conversation flows.

What's actually assessed

Language — accuracy and range across the whole oral, not just the presentation.

Message (visual stimulus) — how relevant your ideas are to the image, and how clearly you link it to the target culture. This one applies to Part 1 only.

Message (conversation) — how well you develop and sustain ideas in Parts 2 and 3.

Interactive skills — how naturally you listen, respond, and keep the conversation moving rather than just answering questions.

Describing an image

A method, not a memorised script

Describe systematically — who's in it, where, what's happening — then move quickly to why it matters: the theme. Spend less time on physical detail than you'd think. Examiners are checking for the link to the theme, not an inventory of everything visible in the photo.

Sur cette photo, on voit...In this photo, we can see...
Au premier plan...In the foreground...
En arrière-plan...In the background...

Il / elle semble...He / she seems, appears to...

The target-culture link

The highest-leverage tip on this page

If you don't explicitly link the stimulus to the target culture in your presentation, you can't be awarded above a certain mark band for Message (visual stimulus) — no matter how well you describe the photo itself. This is one specific, checkable thing examiners look for, and it's the easiest mark on the whole oral to lose entirely just by forgetting it.

ThemeA concrete link you could use
IdentitiesFamily structures and traditions in France or other Francophone countries — Sunday family meals, multigenerational households.
ExperiencesThe French school system (le lycée, le bac), school exchange programmes, gap years.
Human IngenuityFrench inventions, French cinema or music, how technology fits into everyday French life.
Social OrganisationThe French healthcare system, work culture, voting age, or volunteering culture.
Sharing the PlanetFrench environmental policy, recycling systems, environmental issues in Francophone Africa.

Bridge phrases

Dans les pays francophones... · En France, par exemple... · Cela reflète... · C'est similaire à / différent de...

Surviving the conversation

Don't answer in one word

Interactive skills reward development, not just correctness. Extend every answer with a reason, an example, or a related idea rather than stopping at the first correct sentence.

Ask something back

A single question to your teacher — Et vous, qu'en pensez-vous ? — demonstrates genuine interaction, which is exactly what interactive skills is measuring. You don't need to do this often, but doing it at least once shows you're in a conversation, not an interrogation.

Buy yourself time honestly

C'est une bonne question... · Laissez-moi réfléchir... · Je n'avais pas pensé à ça, mais... — these sound natural and buy a second to think, which is far better than silence.

Practice stimuli

Two scenarios written for this guide, each with an image described in detail so you can practise the presentation out loud — describe it yourself first, then compare against the model and notice specifically where the target-culture link happens.

Scenario A — Human Ingenuity

Imagine you're shown this photo

Une photo montre un groupe d'adolescents assis autour d'une table dans un café. Chacun regarde son téléphone portable. Personne ne parle. En arrière-plan, on voit d'autres clients qui font la même chose.

Model presentation · ~1–2 minutes spokenLa technologie et nos relations

Sur cette photo, on voit un groupe d'adolescents assis dans un café. Au premier plan, chaque personne regarde son téléphone et personne ne se parle. L'atmosphère est un peu triste, je trouve.

Cette image est liée au thème de l'ingéniosité humaine, et plus précisément à l'impact de la technologie sur nos relations. Les smartphones nous connectent au monde entier, mais parfois ils nous déconnectent des gens autour de nous.

En France comme ailleurs, ce phénomène existe aussi — certains restaurants français proposent maintenant des réductions aux clients qui rangent leur téléphone pendant le repas. Cela montre que la société commence à réagir à ce problème.

Why this works

Describes the image in two sentences, not ten — then pivots quickly to the theme (Cette image est liée au thème de...). The target-culture link is explicit and concrete (French restaurants offering discounts for put-away phones), not a vague "in France, people also use phones."

Possible follow-up questions

FrenchEnglish
Pourquoi pensez-vous que les jeunes utilisent autant leur téléphone ?Why do you think young people use their phones so much?
Est-ce un problème seulement chez les jeunes ?Is this only a problem among young people?
Que pourrait-on faire pour améliorer la situation ?What could be done to improve the situation?

Scenario B — Social Organisation

Imagine you're shown this photo

Une photo montre un groupe de bénévoles qui nettoient une plage. Ils portent des gants et des sacs poubelles. Il y a des déchets en plastique sur le sable.

Model presentation · ~1–2 minutes spokenLe bénévolat et l'environnement

Sur cette photo, on voit un groupe de bénévoles sur une plage. Ils portent des gants et ramassent des déchets en plastique dans des sacs poubelles. L'atmosphère semble positive — les bénévoles ont l'air motivés.

Cette image représente le thème de l'organisation sociale, et plus précisément le bénévolat et la protection de l'environnement. De plus en plus de jeunes s'engagent dans ce type d'action pour aider leur communauté.

En France, beaucoup d'associations organisent des journées de nettoyage similaires, surtout sur les côtes bretonnes et méditerranéennes. Cela montre l'importance de la responsabilité collective face à la pollution.

Why this works

Same shape as Scenario A: brief description, explicit theme link (le bénévolat et la protection de l'environnement), then a specific, named target-culture reference (Breton and Mediterranean coastal clean-up associations) rather than a generic claim.

Possible follow-up questions

FrenchEnglish
Avez-vous déjà fait du bénévolat ? Racontez.Have you ever volunteered? Tell me about it.
Pourquoi le bénévolat est-il important pour la société, à votre avis ?Why is volunteering important for society, in your opinion?
Quelles autres actions peut-on faire pour protéger l'environnement ?What other actions can be taken to protect the environment?

Tip ↴

  • Practice saying answers out loud, not just writing them — the Individual Oral tests speaking under real time pressure, and reading prepared notes aloud is obvious to an examiner.
  • The target-culture link is the single easiest mark to lose entirely by forgetting it — plan one sentence for it before you even see your two stimuli, so it's automatic regardless of the photo.
  • Silence costs you more than an imperfect answer — a natural filler phrase buys time without breaking the flow of the conversation.
